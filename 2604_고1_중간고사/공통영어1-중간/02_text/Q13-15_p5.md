### Q13. [4.3점]

We have vast forests in this country but not enough to provide all of the wood, all of the wilderness, and all of the accessible recreation that we wish. As soon as we log trees, build roads, or improve trails and campsites, we lose some wilderness. Similarly, we have large amounts of fresh water, but if we use water to grow rice in California, the water consumed cannot be used for drinking water in California cities. If we use fire to help a forest renew itself, we will have air pollution downwind while the fire burns. We have many goals, so we have to make choices about how to allocate our limited resources. The cost of these choices is what we give up — the cost of opportunities lost. Ultimately, every choice we make to use a resource for one purpose ___________________________.

① prevents us from using it for another
② inevitably leads to environmental pollution
③ creates unexpected additional opportunities
④ allows us to take full advantage of natural resources

### Q14. [4.4점]

Not all innovations that improve human living standards show up in revenue and profits. Some instead accrue directly to consumers. Many of the product improvements brought about by technology have the paradoxical effect of reducing industry revenue. To see why, suppose people used to pay $1 to download a song. Then some change comes along so that people can listen to the song while paying only a quarter of what they used to pay. If there is no change in the number of people purchasing the song, revenue will fall by 75 percent. Even if the number of people buying a song doubles with the lower price, revenue will still fall by 50 percent. But people, by which we mean society as a whole, will be much better off. It does not necessarily follow that ____________________.

① a fall in revenue is not by itself evidence that society is worse off
② innovations in technology raise people's standard of living in many ways
③ the price consumers pay for a new invention reflects the total value of its technology
④ an invention is considered successful only when it increases the number of consumers
⑤ a reduction in industry revenue is equated with a decline in society's overall well-being

### Q15. [4.4점]

Knowledge, for science, aims to be proven knowledge, justified by evidence and reason. Nothing is accepted as true unless it has been proved to be so, or there are good reasons to believe that it will be at some point in the future. This reflects the philosophical quest for certainty that goes back to René Descartes, who refused to accept anything that he could not know for certain to be true. He hoped to base all knowledge on self-evident propositions, and thought that reason should take priority over observation. This was quite different from what some other thinkers of his time believed. They thought that all knowledge comes from what we see, hear, and feel, but Descartes trusted the reason more than the senses. Descartes was aware that his senses frequently misled him. The implication of this — a view which had a long history, prior to the rise of science — was that, if the evidence of our senses did not conform to reason, it was likely that they were in error. For Descartes, ___________________________.

① self-evident ideas could only be confirmed through repeated experience
② reason was always more reliable than what our eyes or ears told us
③ the more we observed the world, the closer we got to certain knowledge
④ the senses, when carefully trained, were capable of overcoming their own errors
⑤ both reason and sensory experience were equally necessary for reaching the truth
